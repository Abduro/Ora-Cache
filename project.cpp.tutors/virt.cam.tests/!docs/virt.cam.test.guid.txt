A chamfer is a flat, sloped edge cut into the corner where two surfaces meet, while a facet is any flat surface or plane on a geometric object,
often one of many that make up an entire multi-sided shape like a cut gemstone.

Chamfer
Definition: A specific transitional cut that replaces a sharp 90-degree edge with a single, slanted flat strip.
Placement : Found strictly along the linear edges or corners of a manufactured part.
Use       : Done to remove sharp burrs, make parts safer to handle, and help pins or bolts slide easily into holes.

Facet
Definition: Any distinct, flat planar face on a 3D object.
Placement : Can cover an entire side of an object, not just the edge (e.g., the multiple flat faces of a diamond or a 3D computer model's low-poly surface).
Use       : Used in optics, jewelry cutting (gemstones), and 3D computer graphics to define shape and reflect light.
